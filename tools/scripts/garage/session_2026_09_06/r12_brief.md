#### R12 · Short memory under motion, wide blur while the history is young — NEXT (owner-set 2026-09-06 night, ahead of R5)

**What.** The accumulator keeps up to 64 frames of history and halves that for every texel the picture moves per frame (`reflection_accumulate.rvshader`, `memory = 64 / (1 + moved / slack)`, `slack` = `Probe.y` (1 texel) times 0.25 on mirrors). A floor texel whose reflection slides one texel a frame still averages 32 positions: **that average is the smear the owner sees whenever the camera moves.** After a stop the history restarts short and grainy and takes ~64 frames to fill: **that is the two-second settle.** Every attempt this week nudged the memory and nudged it back because a short history looked noisy. The missing half is spatial: **cap the memory hard under motion (about 8 frames at a texel a frame) and make the blur after the accumulator wide while the history is young, shrinking to nothing as it fills** -- the picture follows the camera within a few frames, and after a stop it is soft but stable and sharpens within half a second. ReBLUR's balance ("the fewer frames behind a pixel, the wider its blur"), SVGF's history-length-guided a-trous, Lumen's reflection history-length bilateral.

**Why.** Owner, 2026-09-06 night, after checking the editor: "is the smearing gone when the camera is moved? no. does the accumulator adapt fast? no, it takes 2 seconds." Measured: the moving floor sits ~19-20 levels from its converged picture on every build since v5 (`smear_metric.py`, f70/f100), and the settle after the dolly is 10.8 / 6.6 / 3.3 levels at 5 / 20 / 60 frames. Nothing else on this list touches those two numbers directly (R10 does, indirectly, by removing the variance that forces the long memory).

**Where.** `reflection_accumulate.rvshader` (the memory block, ~line 410: `slack`, `fewest`, `memory`, `frames`); `reflection_blur.rvshader` (radius `fade * max(1.8 * rough, 0.6 * (1 - rough) * step(0.5, fade))`, `fade = 1 - (frames - 1) / (History.y - 1)`, `kMaxRadius` 3, plane/normal weights); `Renderer3D::BlurReflections` (`push.History.y = 16`); the `ReflectionBlur` pass in `FrameGraphBuilder.cpp` (one pass; a second at stride 2 or 4 is the a-trous ladder the resolve had -- copy that pattern, `Probe.z` as the stride).

**How.**
1. **Measure `moved` parked first.** The accumulator subtracts the jitter, but its own comment says a parked camera still reads "the half pixel this frame's sample sits from the centre". Add a debug view of `moved` (R6's pattern: a scale of 4 texels) or dump it through the `reflection-choice` channel for one run. The cap below must sit above the parked floor.
2. **The cap:** `memory = mix(64, kMovingMemory, smoothstep(kStillTexels, kMovingTexels, moved))` with `kMovingMemory = 8`, `kStillTexels` just above the parked floor from step 1, `kMovingTexels` about 1.5; keep `fewest` (mirrors 1) and the `moved / slack` division as the taper between. Do not touch the silhouette rule or the bound.
3. **The blur:** radius in texels per axis from the history length, `young = 1 - clamp((frames - 1) / (kBlurFrames - 1))` with `kBlurFrames` 32, `radius = kYoungRadius * young * young` (about 16 texels at one frame, 4 at half, 0 at 32) -- **bounded by the lobe's footprint per axis** so a chrome pole is never blurred past its own lobe (reuse the resolve's footprint: hit distance times `kLobeWidth * alpha` over the texel's world size, the anisotropy included), and scaled by the gloss window so mirrors get none. Reach it with the a-trous ladder (strides 1, 2, 4; three passes of 7 taps, or two of 9) rather than one wide kernel; Gaussian weights, the existing plane and normal weights kept so the blur never crosses a silhouette (or the edge flicker returns). The blur reads the accumulated picture and writes the composite's input; **the history itself stays sharp**, so there is no feedback and the blur cannot smear over time.
4. Nothing in the composite or TAA changes.
